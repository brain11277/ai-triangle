# Glossary

Every term the framework uses, one definition each. Each entry is written to stand alone, so
it can be quoted without the surrounding page.

---

**The AI Triangle**
The AI Triangle is a practitioner-facing project management triangle, published by Brian Rain
in 2026, in which Quality is fixed and means the human's discernment is still intact, and the
three trade variables are continuous improvement disciplines the practitioner imposes on
themselves: continuous improvement of self, of their AI tools, and of new AI.

**CI**
Continuous improvement. *Not to be confused with* Continuous Integration, which is what "CI"
means to most of the engineering audience this framework addresses. The framework always
expands the term on first use for this reason, and the diagram deliberately carries plain
nouns rather than the abbreviation, because a graphic travels detached from the page that
defines its terms.

**CI of Self**
Continuous improvement of self. Your discernment, your AI literacy, your judgment about when
to trust output and when to overrule it. Maintained by the Weekly Self-Review Block.

**CI of Your AI Tools**
Continuous improvement of your AI tools. The discipline of treating your prompts, agents,
sub-agents and automations as versioned artifacts that decay without active maintenance.
Maintained by the Agent Refactor Sprint.

**CI of New AI**
Continuous improvement of new AI. The bounded, deliberate practice of scanning, testing and
triaging new tools, platform features and automation patterns as they appear, without
descending into infinite chasing. Maintained by the Scan-and-Triage Cadence.

**Quality (in the AI Triangle)**
The fixed constraint. In this framework Quality means the human's discernment is still intact,
not that the artifact conforms to a specification. The artifact looks good is a different
claim from the human producing the artifact still knows what good looks like.

**Weekly Self-Review Block**
A recurring 30 to 60 minute appointment spent reviewing your own recent AI-assisted output
specifically for discernment failures. Its output is not a deliverable. If it produces work,
it has become a production block and stopped being a self-review block.

**Agent Refactor Sprint**
A recurring 90 minute session in which the highest-frequency assets in your personal AI stack
are re-architected based on what running them in production taught you. Distinguished from
cosmetic cleanup by the requirement that every recorded change names the failure that prompted
it.

**Scan-and-Triage Cadence**
A bounded weekly window, typically an hour with a hard stop, split into scanning what is new,
testing exactly one thing against a real task, and dispositioning everything else. The hard
stop is the discipline.

**Not now**
The third disposition, alongside yes and no, for a new tool or capability. A real not-now
carries a revisit date and a trigger condition. Without those it is a way of avoiding a
decision rather than making one.

**The triage**
The act of dispositioning every scanned item as no, yes, or not now. Named as the skill of the
vertex, because scanning is easy and testing is bounded, while deciding not to act is the part
that most practitioners skip.

**Trigger condition**
The observable event that makes a not-now item worth revisiting. "Revisit when my inventory
has a row this would replace" is a trigger condition. "Revisit in Q3" is a calendar entry, and
a ledger built from calendar entries becomes a graveyard.

**The accept log**
The durable artifact of CI of Self. Three columns recording, for each place you took model
output as-is: what you accepted, what you would have written yourself, and why you did not
write it. The third column is the one that does the work.

**The AI stack inventory**
The durable artifact of CI of Your AI Tools. A list of every prompt, agent and automation you
rely on, with how often it runs, when it last changed, and what breaks if it is wrong. Kept as
a CSV rather than prose because it is living data.

**The not-now ledger**
The durable artifact of CI of New AI. A record of everything you scanned and deliberately did
not adopt, with the revisit date and trigger condition for each. An item whose revisit date
passes twice with no decision converts to a hard no.

**Over-prompting as a discernment hide**
A failure mode in which a practitioner whose CI of Self has atrophied compensates by writing
increasingly elaborate prompts. The prompt does the thinking, the output looks great, and the
practitioner is genuinely worse than they were six months ago and cannot tell.

**Tool sprawl masquerading as adoption**
A failure mode in which CI of New AI consumes all available time, CI of existing tools rots,
and CI of Self never moves. Output quality stays flat or declines, masked by the dopamine of
trying new things.

**Cognitive offloading**
Delegating a cognitive task to an external system rather than performing it. The mechanism the
CI of Self research anchors are concerned with. Offloading is not inherently harmful; the
framework's concern is the unnoticed, unmaintained kind.

**Shape**
The named result of an assessment. One of five: Balanced, Collapsing, Tilting, Lopsided,
Uneven. The framework names shapes rather than scores deliberately, because two very different
distributions can produce the same total, and the distribution is the finding. No total is
ever computed.

**Observer pass**
An optional variant of the assessment in which someone who sees your output scores the CI of
Self items about you. The gap between your score and theirs is the actual signal. Requires
consent, and must never be collected by a manager.
