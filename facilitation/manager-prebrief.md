# Manager prebrief

Send this **before** the session to anyone who might reasonably ask for the results
afterwards. Preventing the request is far easier than declining it, and declining it after the
fact makes the facilitator look evasive rather than principled.

Copy the message below, adjust the first line, and send it.

---

## The message

> Subject: The AI Triangle session on *(date)*, and what I can share afterwards
>
> Hello,
>
> I am running a 90-minute AI Triangle session with the team on *(date)*. It is a practitioner
> self-assessment: each person rates their own practice across three continuous improvement
> disciplines and commits to one change.
>
> I want to be clear in advance about one thing, because it is the difference between the
> session working and being a waste of everyone's time.
>
> **Individual scores are not collected, and I will not be able to share them.** This is not a
> confidentiality preference. The instrument is entirely self-report, and the research it is
> built on found that the practitioners most at risk are precisely the ones most likely to
> rate themselves generously. The moment a score carries any consequence, people give the safe
> answer rather than the accurate one, the data becomes noise, and it stays noise for every
> future run. There is no version of this where the numbers are both collected and useful.
>
> **What I can share, and will:**
>
> 1. **The shape tally.** Each person's result resolves to one of five words: Balanced,
>    Collapsing, Tilting, Lopsided, Uneven. I will share the counts, unattributed. If five of
>    seven people come back Tilting, that tells you something real about the last quarter, and
>    it is more actionable than a list of scores.
>
> 2. **What came out of the failure-modes discussion.** Twenty minutes of the session asks
>    where our polished output might be hiding a thinking gap, and what we adopted recently
>    that nobody has maintained since. That produces a concrete list about the work, and it is
>    usually the most useful thing to come out of the session.
>
> 3. **The commitment-kept rate at 90 days.** Everyone leaves with one recurring block on
>    their calendar. At the re-run I can tell you what proportion were still being held. That
>    is the actual measure of whether this worked.
>
> Those three are genuinely more informative than individual scores, and none of them requires
> anyone to misrepresent their own practice to protect themselves.
>
> Happy to talk it through if useful.

---

## If the request comes anyway

Do not renegotiate. The three shareable signals are the answer, and they are a real answer
rather than a deflection. Offer the shape tally immediately, which usually satisfies the
underlying need, because what a manager normally wants is a read on the team, not a ranking of
individuals.

If the request is explicitly for attributable scores and will not move, the honest position is
that the session cannot produce them, and that running it under those terms would produce
numbers that are wrong rather than numbers that are private. Then offer to run the
failure-modes discussion as a standalone forty-five minute session, which requires no
self-assessment, surfaces genuinely useful material about the work, and has none of this
problem.

## If you are the manager

Running this with your own direct reports is fine, and common, provided the three rules hold
and you go first with your own lowest vertex. The thing that breaks it is not your presence in
the room. It is a score reaching a document that outlives the session.

Do not run the observer pass with your own reports. That one requires a peer.
