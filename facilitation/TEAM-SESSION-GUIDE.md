# Running the AI Triangle with a team

90 minutes. For a team lead, a coach, or anyone who has been asked to "run that triangle
thing."

---

## Read this before you plan the session

**This is a framework of disciplines a practitioner imposes on themselves.** Run as a team
assessment, it becomes a performance review. That is not a tone problem to be managed with a
friendly introduction. It is a mechanism problem, and it is fatal:

The instrument is self-report. The moment a score carries any consequence, the honest answer
and the safe answer come apart, everyone gives the safe one, and every subsequent run is
worthless. You do not get to undo it by promising to be careful next time. The team now knows
what the exercise is for.

So the session does not ask people to be honest. It is **built so that dishonesty has no
purpose**, because nobody ever says a number.

The three rules, which are structural rather than cultural:

1. **Individual scores are never spoken.** Not in the round, not in the corridor.
2. **Nothing is written on a shared surface.** No numbers on the whiteboard, in a doc, or in a
   thread.
3. **Nothing is collected.** Not by you, not later, not in aggregate "just for the team view."

If you cannot commit to all three, do not run the session. Send people the printable and let
them do it alone, which works fine.

---

## Before the day

- Send [`manager-prebrief.md`](manager-prebrief.md) to anyone above you who might ask for the
  results. Send it **before** the session, not after the request. Preventing the ask is much
  easier than declining it.
- Print the assessment, one per person plus two spares.
- Book a room with a whiteboard. Write the five shape words on it in advance: Balanced,
  Collapsing, Tilting, Lopsided, Uneven.
- Fill the instrument in yourself, honestly, beforehand. You will need your own lowest vertex
  in the first ten minutes.

**Materials:** printed instruments, pens, a whiteboard, a timer everyone can see. Pens rather
than laptops for the assessment portion, because an open laptop turns a reflective task into a
multitasked one, and because nothing typed can be accidentally shared.

---

## The 90 minutes

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:10 | **Frame** | The third triangle, one slide. Quality is fixed and means discernment intact. Then the no-numbers script, out loud. |
| 0:10 to 0:25 | **Silent assessment** | Everyone fills the instrument alone. Laptops closed. You fill it in too, again. |
| 0:25 to 0:35 | **Private interpretation** | Each person reads their own band text and writes one sentence: the vertex I am investing in, and the first block I will put on my calendar. Nobody reads this out. |
| 0:35 to 0:55 | **Shape round** | Around the room, each person names **only the shape word**. Optionally which vertex. Never a number. You tally shapes on the board. |
| 0:55 to 1:15 | **Failure modes, applied** | Two prompts against this team's real work. Captured on the board. |
| 1:15 to 1:25 | **Commitments** | Calendars open. Each person creates one recurring block, in the room. You wait for it. |
| 1:25 to 1:30 | **Re-run date** | 90 days. On the calendar now. |

### Frame, 10 minutes

One slide on the lineage, one on the three vertices, one on what Quality means here. Do not
teach the whole framework; people can read it afterwards.

Then say this, in roughly these words, out loud, before anyone picks up a pen:

> Nobody says their numbers today. Not to me, not to each other. Nothing gets collected,
> nothing goes in a doc, and this is not an input to any review. The only thing we will say
> out loud is one word each. I have already told my manager they are not getting the results.
>
> This works because it is self-report, and self-report only works when it is safe. If you
> shade your answers today, the only person you cost is you.

If someone asks whether you are really not collecting it, the answer is yes, really, and the
prebrief is the evidence.

### Silent assessment, 15 minutes

Laptops closed. Fill it in yourself rather than watching the room; you need your own result,
and filling it in signals that you are inside the exercise rather than administering it.

Nudge once at the halfway mark: "answer items 1, 6, 7, 9 and 10 with your calendar open if you
can."

### Private interpretation, 10 minutes

Each person converts their raw scores, finds their shape, reads that band's text in
[`../assessment/SCORING.md`](../assessment/SCORING.md), and writes **one sentence**: the vertex
they are investing in, and the first block going on the calendar.

Print the five band interpretations and put them on the table, or have people read them on
their phones. This is the only screen time in the session.

### Shape round, 20 minutes

Each person says **one word**. "Tilting." Optionally: "Tilting, and it is tools."

You tally on the board. The tally is the team-level artifact and it is the only one.

**Go first, and name your own lowest vertex.** This is the single most load-bearing thing you
do all session. A facilitator who goes first with a genuine weakness sets the floor; a
facilitator who goes last, or goes first with "Balanced," turns the round into a status game
within about ninety seconds.

If the tally clusters, say so and move on. Five Tiltings in a team of seven is a finding about
the quarter, not about five people.

### Failure modes applied, 20 minutes

Two prompts. These are about the work, not the people.

1. **Where does a polished artifact hide a thinking gap in what we ship?**
2. **What did we adopt in the last two quarters that nobody has refactored since?**

Capture on the board. Question 2 usually produces a concrete list within five minutes, and the
list is frequently the most useful output of the whole session.

### Commitments, 10 minutes

Calendars open. Everyone creates **one recurring event**. In the room.

Wait for it. The room goes quiet and it feels slightly awkward and that is the mechanism: an
intention formed in a meeting does not survive the walk back to the desk, and a recurring
calendar event does.

Say plainly: one block, not three. A person leaving with three new recurring commitments keeps
none of them.

### Re-run, 5 minutes

90 days, scheduled now. Compare shape words, not numbers, and say why: self-report drift over
three months makes a one-point change meaningless, while Collapsing to Lopsided is a real
change in practice.

## Failure modes in the room

**The score becomes a status game.** Symptom: someone volunteers a high number, or says
"Balanced, obviously." Move: restate the no-numbers rule once, without irritation, then name
your own lowest vertex again. Do not argue with them. The behaviour needs an audience and the
rule removes it.

**The senior person answers first and everyone calibrates.** Move: reverse seniority order in
the shape round, or make it write-then-reveal. Decide this before the session if there is a
big seniority spread in the room.

**Everyone lands on Balanced.** The instrument was filled in optimistically and the room
probably knows it. Move: offer the observer pass as a voluntary ten-minute extension, in pairs
people choose themselves, with explicit consent. **This is the highest-risk variant in the
guide.** Do not run it in a team with an active trust problem, in a team that has had recent
performance conversations, or where anyone in the pairing writes anyone else's review. If in
doubt, skip it and let the 90-day re-run do the work.

**It turns into a tool-evaluation meeting.** Symptom: forty minutes on which model is better.
Move: name it. "This is a scan window, and it is a different meeting." Put the topic on a
visible not-now list and offer to book it.

**Commitments are aspirational.** Symptom: "I will try to review more." Move: nobody leaves
until a recurring event exists. Ask to hear the notification sound if you have to; it lands as
a joke and it works.

**The manager asks for the numbers afterwards.** Move: this should already be prevented by
[`manager-prebrief.md`](manager-prebrief.md). If it is not, the two things you can legitimately
share are the **shape tally** and, at 90 days, the **commitment-kept rate**. Both are
aggregate, neither is attributable, and both are more useful to a manager than a list of
scores would have been.

## Variants

**45-minute short version.** Frame (5), assessment (15), private interpretation (10),
commitments (10), re-run date (5). Drop the shape round and the failure modes. The commitments
block is the one that cannot be cut.

**Async.** Send the printable individually. Shape words only in a thread, one word each.
Commitments posted as "block created." No numbers in the thread, and say so in the first
message, because a thread is a shared surface that persists.

**One-to-one coaching, 20 minutes.** The observer pass is natural and safe here in a way it is
not in a group, provided you are not the person's manager. Assessment, shape, one commitment.

---

## What this session is not for

Deciding tooling. Comparing people. Generating a team score. Reporting upward. Identifying who
needs development. If any of those is the reason you were asked to run it, the honest move is
to say the instrument cannot do it, and offer the failure-modes discussion on its own, which
genuinely can.
