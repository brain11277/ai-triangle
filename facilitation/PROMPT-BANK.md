# Discussion prompts

For the failure-modes block, and for coaching conversations.

The distinction that runs through this whole page: **prompts that point at the artifact work;
prompts that point at the person do not.** Not because pointing at people is unkind, but
because the framework's central claim is that the artifact does not reveal the problem. A
question aimed at a person invites a defence of their competence. A question aimed at the work
invites an observation, and observations are what the session is for.

---

## Prompts that work

**On discernment and output**

1. What would a stranger reading our output notice that we cannot?
2. Which of our recurring deliverables would be identical if we all stopped thinking about it?
3. When did we last change our minds about something because of what we found while writing
   it, rather than before?
4. What is the last thing we shipped that someone would have pushed back on a year ago?

**On tools**

5. What is the oldest prompt anyone here still uses daily?
6. What do we have that everyone depends on and nobody owns?
7. What did we build to work around a limitation that no longer exists?
8. If one of our agents started producing slightly worse output tomorrow, how long until
   someone noticed, and how would they notice?

**On new AI**

9. What did we adopt last quarter that nobody has opened since?
10. What are we all quietly assuming we should be using, and has anyone actually tested it?
11. What is the last thing we decided *not* to adopt, and was that decision written down
    anywhere?

**On the practice itself**

12. Which of the three is easiest for us to do, and is that because it matters most or because
    it is the most enjoyable?

Question 8 is the most reliable one in this list. It produces a concrete answer, it is
impossible to answer defensively, and the answer is almost always "quite a long time," which
is the point being made.

## Prompts that backfire

**Who scored lowest?**
Breaks the no-numbers rule and converts the session into a ranking. Once asked, the rule is
gone for everyone in the room, including for people who were not asked.

**Who is over-prompting?**
Asks people to accuse themselves in public. Nobody will, and the ones who should will be the
least likely to.

**Should we standardize on one tool?**
A procurement question wearing a practice question's clothes. It is genuinely interesting,
which is exactly why it consumes the remaining time.

**Does everyone agree they need to improve here?**
Manufactures consent for something nobody said. The commitments block already handles this,
individually and privately.

**Why is your score lower than last quarter?**
Treats self-report as a metric, punishes honest re-rating, and guarantees inflated answers
next time. This single question can end the practice for a team.

**What is the team's overall score?**
There is no total, by design: two very different practices produce the same sum. See
[`../assessment/SCORING.md`](../assessment/SCORING.md).

**Can we make this part of the development plan?**
Well-meant and fatal. The instrument stops working the moment it has consequences attached.
Offer the failure-modes discussion instead, which can be part of anything.

## If the room goes quiet

Silence after prompt 1 or 2 is normal and usually productive. Wait longer than is comfortable
before rescuing it.

If it is still quiet after twenty seconds, answer it yourself about your own work, concretely
and with something real. The same mechanism as going first in the shape round: the floor gets
set by whoever is willing to be first, and if that is not you, it will be nobody.
