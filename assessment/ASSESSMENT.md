# The AI Triangle self-assessment

Twelve items, four per vertex. Ten minutes. Pen and paper is better than a screen for this,
for reasons given below.

A printable one-page version with the plotting grid is at
[`printable/ai-triangle-assessment.html`](printable/ai-triangle-assessment.html). Open it in
any browser and print it. Scoring is in [`SCORING.md`](SCORING.md).

---

## How to answer

Every item is about the **last 30 days**. Not your intentions, not a typical month, not what
you would do if the quarter were calmer. The last 30 days.

| Answer | Score |
|---|---|
| Yes | 2 |
| Partly | 1 |
| No | 0 |

"Partly" means you did some of it, or did it inconsistently. If you are arguing with yourself
about whether something counts, it is a partly.

Each vertex totals 0 to 8.

The items are deliberately behavioural and time-bounded rather than attitudinal. "I value
continuous improvement" is unfalsifiable. "I held a self-review block at least three times in
the last 30 days" is checkable against your own calendar, which is the point.

---

## CI of Self

**1.** I held a self-review block at least three times in the last 30 days.

**2.** In the last 30 days I named at least one specific output I accepted that I should have
challenged.

**3.** In the last 30 days I produced at least one piece of work of consequence where the
first draft was mine, not a model's.

**4.** I can name, right now and without looking it up, one thing I am worse at than I was six
months ago.

> Item 4 is the hardest item on the instrument and the most informative. A confident "no"
> here, from someone scoring well elsewhere, is worth paying attention to.

**CI of Self raw score: ___ / 8**

---

## CI of Your AI Tools

**5.** My highest-frequency prompts and agents live in files outside the chat window.

**6.** At least one of them has a change I made in the last 30 days that I could point to in
writing.

**7.** I retired or merged at least one prompt or agent in the last 90 days.

**8.** I can name the specific failure that prompted the last change to my most-used asset.

> Item 8 separates maintenance from tinkering. A change with no prompting failure behind it is
> the cosmetic cleanup the framework explicitly says this vertex is not.

**CI of Your AI Tools raw score: ___ / 8**

---

## CI of New AI

**9.** I held a bounded scan window at least three times in the last 30 days, and it ended
when the time ended.

**10.** I tested exactly one new capability against a real task in the last 30 days, with a
success criterion written first.

**11.** I keep a written not-now list, and at least one item on it has a revisit date.

**12.** In the last 30 days I said not-now to something I was genuinely excited about.

> Item 12 is the triage item. Saying not-now to something you did not want anyway is not a
> skill. This is the one that measures the discipline the vertex is named for.

**CI of New AI raw score: ___ / 8**

---

## Then

Convert each raw score to the 1 to 5 axis and find your shape using
[`SCORING.md`](SCORING.md). If you are using the printable, plot the three values on the grid
and connect them with a pen.

Drawing the shape by hand rather than watching a widget draw it is not decoration. The
interactive version produces the answer in under a second, and an answer produced that fast is
easy to look at without absorbing. Marking three ticks and connecting them takes about
forty seconds, and people report that the lopsided ones land differently on paper.

## What this instrument cannot see

This section is here because leaving it out would be a small act of exactly the dishonesty the
framework is about.

**It is entirely self-report, and self-report is the thing the underlying research says
degrades.** Lee et al. (2025), which is one of the anchors of CI of Self, found that
confidence in GenAI predicted *less* critical thinking while self-confidence in one's own
expertise predicted *more*. The profile most at risk is a practitioner with high trust in the
tool and low task-specific self-confidence, and that is precisely the profile most likely to
read these twelve items generously. The instrument is weakest exactly where the problem is
worst.

**There is no way to fix this from inside the instrument.** More carefully worded items do not
help, because the failure is not comprehension. Adding a social desirability control would
make it longer without making it honest.

**What does help, partially.** Three things, in order of how much they cost you.

1. **Answer against evidence, not memory.** Items 1, 6, 7, 9 and 10 are checkable. Open your
   calendar and your files before you answer them. Most of the inflation in this instrument
   lives in those five items and most of it disappears under a thirty second check.

2. **Re-read item 4 last.** If you answered item 4 "no" and everything else "yes," that
   combination is itself a finding. A practitioner genuinely maintaining their discernment
   usually has an answer to item 4.

3. **The observer pass.** Ask someone who actually sees your output to score items 1 through 4
   about you. The **gap** between your score and theirs is the real signal, and it is more
   informative than either score alone.

**The observer pass has rules, and they are not optional.** It requires explicit consent from
both people. It must never be collected, stored, or seen by anyone's manager. Do not run it in
a team with an active trust problem, where it will be read as surveillance no matter how it is
framed. If you cannot meet those conditions, skip it. A missing signal is better than a
poisoned one.

---

## What a score is not

It is not a performance measure, an input to a review, a hiring signal, or a prediction of
your output quality. If a number from this instrument has ended up in a document that outlives
the session, something has gone wrong. See
[`../framework/FAILURE-MODES.md`](../framework/FAILURE-MODES.md), failure mode 6.

There is also no total. The three raw scores are never summed, and
[`SCORING.md`](SCORING.md) explains why: the same total can describe two completely different
practices, and the distribution is the entire finding.
