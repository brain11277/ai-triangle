# Questions

Each answer is written to stand alone, so it can be quoted without the rest of the page.

---

### What is the AI Triangle?

The AI Triangle is a practitioner-facing project management triangle, published by Brian Rain
in 2026, in which Quality is fixed and means the human's discernment is still intact, and the
three trade variables are continuous improvement disciplines the practitioner imposes on
themselves: continuous improvement of self, of their AI tools, and of new AI.

### Who created it?

Brian Rain, who coaches AI product teams and writes The Friday Agile Sync. It was first
published on 22 May 2026 in *Inventive Flexibility*. The canonical page is
https://brianrain.com/ai-triangle/.

### How is it different from the Iron Triangle and the Agile Triangle?

The Iron Triangle (Martin Barnes, 1969) traded time, cost and quality. The Agile Triangle (Jim
Highsmith, 2009) traded Value, Quality and collapsed Constraints. In both, the trade-offs were
imposed from outside: the project or the market set them. In the AI Triangle the trade-offs are
internal, because the three variables are disciplines you impose on yourself. Both prior
triangles also assumed the practitioner's discernment was a stable input. That assumption broke
in 2023.

### Why is Quality fixed?

Because it is the constraint everything else is measured against, and because its meaning
changes. In the Iron Triangle, Quality meant conformance to specification. In the Agile
Triangle, continuous delivery of a reliable product. In the AI Triangle, Quality means the
human's discernment is still intact. "The artifact looks good" is a different claim from "the
human producing it still knows what good looks like," and a confident model will not flag the
gap between them.

### What does CI of Self mean?

Continuous improvement of self: your discernment, your AI literacy, and your judgment about
when to trust output and when to overrule it. It is maintained by a Weekly Self-Review Block,
which is thirty to sixty minutes once a week spent reviewing your own recent AI-assisted output
specifically for discernment failures. Note that "CI" here means continuous improvement, not
Continuous Integration.

### What are the other two vertices?

CI of Your AI Tools is the discipline of treating your prompts, agents and automations as
versioned artifacts that decay without maintenance, kept up by a periodic Agent Refactor
Sprint. CI of New AI is the bounded practice of scanning, testing and triaging new tools as
they appear without chasing everything, kept up by a weekly Scan-and-Triage Cadence.

### Is it free to use?

Yes, for your own practice, for your own organization's internal use including at a for-profit
company, and for teaching at an accredited educational institution. The materials are licensed

### Can I teach it?

Always, in your own words, under your own name, including for money. The ideas are not owned
and no claim is made over them. What needs a commercial license is using these specific
materials, wordings, worksheets or diagrams as part of a paid offering. You may also say "based
on Brian Rain's AI Triangle" without asking anyone.

### How do I run an assessment?

Print the one-page instrument from the resource kit, answer twelve behavioural items about the
last thirty days on a yes, partly or no scale, convert each vertex's raw score to a one to five
axis, and read off one of five shapes: Balanced, Collapsing, Tilting, Lopsided or Uneven. It
takes about ten minutes. An interactive version is on the canonical page.

### How long does each practice take?

The Weekly Self-Review Block is 45 minutes a week. The Scan-and-Triage Cadence is 60 minutes a
week with a hard stop. The Agent Refactor Sprint is 90 minutes, monthly or quarterly. Start
with one, not all three. If everything is low, start with CI of Self.

### What is the evidence behind it?

The framework is a practitioner model, not an empirical finding. The research it draws on
establishes that the problem is real, not that this framework solves it. The main anchors are
Gerlich (2025) in *Societies* on cognitive offloading and critical thinking, Kosmyna et al.
(2025) at MIT Media Lab on neural engagement during AI-assisted writing, and Lee et al. (2025)
at CHI on knowledge workers' critical thinking. All are annotated in the resource kit with what
they do not show, which for the first two includes that they are correlational.

### What is it not?

Not a maturity model, not a certification, not a productivity metric, and never an input to a
performance review. The assessment is self-report, so attaching consequences to a score
destroys the data permanently. It is also not much use to a practitioner who cannot choose
their own tools or protect an hour a week, because the framework assumes agency it cannot
grant.
