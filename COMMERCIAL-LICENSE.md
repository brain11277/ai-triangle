- Use any of this for your own practice: the assessment, the worksheets, the diagrams, the

- Quote it, critique it, or publicly disagree with it.
- Read, fork, modify and share the widget code for non-production use.

Whatever you produce by practising the framework is yours. The license claims nothing over
your self-review notes, your agent inventory, your not-now ledger, or any decision you make

## You do need a commercial license to

- Deliver consulting, coaching or training built on these materials for a fee.
- Sell a product, a course, or a hosted tool built on them.
- Offer the widget, or a derivative of it, to third parties as a service or a bundled tool.

The test is not whether money is involved anywhere near you. It is whether the materials are
part of what is being sold.

## Teaching the ideas is always free

You never need a license to teach the underlying ideas in your own words, under your own
name, including for money. The ideas are not owned. What needs a license is using Brian's
specific materials, wording, diagrams or code as part of a paid offering. See
[TRADEMARK.md](TRADEMARK.md), which explains the distinction and what you may still call it.

## Asking

Open an issue titled "Commercial license" describing what you want to build, or use the
contact address in the repository profile. Terms are negotiated case by case, and small
independent operators should expect that to be reflected in the terms.

If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, ask before you build. The answer is free
and usually short.
