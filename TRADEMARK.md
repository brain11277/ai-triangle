# The name

This page is about the words "The AI Triangle" and nothing else. It is deliberately narrow.
Most of what people worry about needing permission for is on the free side of every line
below.

## What is claimed

"The AI Triangle" is used by Brian Rain as the name of the framework described in this
repository. It was first published on 2026-05-22 in The Friday Agile Sync on *Inventive
Flexibility*, and the canonical page is https://brianrain.com/ai-triangle/.

The claim is an unregistered, common-law one, arising from use. No trademark application has
been filed. The mark is written `The AI Triangle™`, never with `®`, because `®` would be
false.

Neither the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license on the materials nor the BSL 1.1 license on the code
grants any rights in the name. Both license texts say so themselves. This page is the whole
of what is asked.

## The one ask

Do not use the name in a way that suggests Brian Rain authored, endorsed, certified, or
accredited your offering when he did not.

Concretely, and these are the only examples that matter:

- No "Certified AI Triangle Practitioner", or any other certification, credential or badge.
- No "Official AI Triangle Training", "AI Triangle Institute", "AI Triangle Foundation", or
  any name that reads as the source of the framework rather than a user of it.
- No logo, domain or product name that a reasonable person would take as this project's own.

That is the entire ask. It is about not misrepresenting the source, not about controlling
who gets to discuss the work.

## What is not claimed

This section matters more than the one above, and it is written as its own heading so it
cannot be skimmed past.

**The ideas are not owned.** Copyright does not protect ideas, and no claim is made over
them here. A three-vertex model of practitioner continuous improvement, a fixed quality
constraint, the argument that discernment degrades under cognitive offloading, the claim that
prompts and agents are versioned artifacts that decay: none of that is ownable and none of it
is claimed. You may teach these concepts, disagree with them publicly, build a better version,
publish a competing framework, or charge money for training on the underlying ideas, in your
own words and under your own name. You do not need permission and you do not need to tell
anyone.

**Two narrow things are protected, and they are different from each other.** The *expression*
is protected by copyright: the specific wording of the specification, the worksheets, the
assessment items, the diagrams and the widget code. The *name* is protected by trademark, as
a source identifier only. Neither of those is a claim on the concept.

**Nominative use is fine and is not asking permission.** You may use the name to refer to the
thing. All of these are fine, and none of them require a license or a conversation:

- "based on Brian Rain's AI Triangle"
- "compatible with the AI Triangle"
- "a critique of the AI Triangle"
- "why I think the AI Triangle is wrong"
- "we run an AI Triangle assessment internally"
- "AI Triangle, adapted" on a slide you made yourself

**No claim is made over the English words.** This page does not assert rights in the word
"AI", the word "triangle", or the phrase in the abstract. It is a descriptive phrase and
therefore a weak mark, which is stated here plainly rather than left for someone else to
point out. If you independently use those words for something unrelated, this page has
nothing to say about it. No rights are asserted in any other field of use.

## Where this sits next to the license

The license governs whether you may copy and adapt the materials. This page governs whether
you may use the name to describe what you are offering. They are separate questions and they
can have different answers.

A paid course that teaches the ideas in the instructor's own words, and says "based on Brian
Rain's AI Triangle", needs no copyright license because it copies nothing, and the nominative
use of the name is fine. A paid course built from these worksheets needs a commercial license,
because of the copying, regardless of what it is called.

## If you are unsure

Open an issue. Describing what you want to do is usually enough, and the answer is normally
"that is fine, go ahead."
